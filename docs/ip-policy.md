# Intellectual property and publication policy

This records the project's chosen open-publication approach. It is not legal
advice, a title opinion, a patent search, or trademark clearance. Qualified
counsel should review rights before commercial deployment or registration.

## Copyright and reuse

Authors retain copyright in their contributions. Code is distributed under
[Apache-2.0](../LICENSES/Apache-2.0.txt); documentation/specification and diagrams
under [CC-BY-4.0](../LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt). Keep license/attribution notices and
identify changes as required by the applicable license.

Both licenses permit commercial reuse. They do not prevent competing
implementations or grant exclusive ownership of an internet architecture.
Copyright generally protects expression, not ideas or functionality.

Apache-2.0 includes a defined contributor patent grant and a patent-litigation
termination provision. Its grant is limited to the claims and contributions
described by that license; it is not universal patent clearance. CC-BY-4.0
does not license patent or trademark rights.

## Contributions and dependency rights

Use the [contributor terms](../CLA.md); no copyright assignment or blanket
relicensing permission is requested. Contributors must have authority to
submit their work. Broader dual licensing would require appropriate rights
from the relevant authors; this scaffold does not create them automatically.

Review the actual license of every integrated implementation. WireGuard-related
implementations can carry different licenses; using a protocol does not mean
its implementation can be copied into Apache-licensed source. No WireGuard or
third-party cryptographic implementation is vendored here. Keep required notices
and satisfy compatibility/distribution obligations before integration.

## Name, brand and certification

"Worldwide Identity Web" and "WIW" are selected project names, not claims of
registered or exclusively enforceable marks. A GitHub/web search is not a
trademark clearance search. Distinctiveness and conflicting rights require a
jurisdiction- and class-specific review, potentially covering DPMA, EUIPO,
WIPO and other relevant registries.

See [TRADEMARKS.md](../TRADEMARKS.md). No certification program or "WIW Certified"
mark is currently offered. A future program needs conformance criteria,
independent governance and legal review; do not present a preview as certified.

## Patents and public disclosure

The project owner has chosen public publication. An enabling public disclosure
may become prior art and may impair the author's own ability to obtain patents.
Grace periods vary; European patent law generally does not provide a broad
grace period for an inventor's voluntary publication.

Publication does not guarantee that others cannot obtain patents, invalidate
existing patents, or establish freedom to operate. A defensive-publication
strategy depends on substance, accessibility, timing and applicable law.

Do not submit third-party confidential information, unpublished inventions you
lack authority to disclose, identity documents, keys or other secrets.

## Service names

WIW name-registration policy is not implemented. DNS control and a verified
operator identity would not by themselves resolve trademark ownership or name
disputes. A future registry needs reviewable claims, transfer/recovery rules,
confusable-name handling, a dispute process and appeals.
